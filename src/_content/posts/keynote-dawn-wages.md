### We're honored to announce Dawn Wages as a keynote speaker for DjangoCon US 2026

We had the opportunity to sit down with Dawn  and ask her a few questions about her work, her journey through the Django community, and what attendees can expect from her keynote at DjangoCon US 2026.

## How have Star Trek, Octavia Butler, and Afrofuturism influenced the way you approach leadership and community building?

>  This is the crux of the presentation! I'm shaking out the nuances and how I will be telling the story on stage, but the long and short of is: (1) reclaiming agency, (2) understanding history as a context for the present (3) not being afraid of fear (3) leaning on and building with your peers / contemporaries. 

## Your keynote asks, “Is there room for me on the Bridge?” How has the meaning of that question changed throughout your career?

>  Thanks for this question! This one has remained eerily consistent which is I think the conceit I will try to make on stage. Despite sci-fi and afrofuturism making me feel like I have something worthwhile to offer; 10+ years later I still feel like I'm met with signals that there is not room for me. There are not audible invitations to lead. It is different that imposter syndrome because I do feel capable in these spaces, but when I enter rooms as a Black woman my capabilities must be credentialed, verified and then reviewed; and even after still held under scrutiny. What does my space or the space of someone like me look like in 2026 and beyond. Is there space for us?

## What does it mean to “build worlds worth living in” within open-source communities?

> Anti-racist, not just gender-inclusive, but gender expansive, accessible, anti-fascist, community-oriented, prioritizing the voices of the marginalized first, intersectional.

## You have served the Python community in many roles, including organizer, maintainer, speaker, entrepreneur, and PSF Board Chair. Which experiences have most shaped your approach to mentorship and governance?

> They've all shaped me in different ways and its hard to tease out one or the other. Being on the Python Software Foundation Board was a milestone in my career and marked a big shift in legitimacy. But then that begs the question: Was it a symbol of legitimacy that I already embodied, or was the legitimacy earned due to the investments people saw me making on a larger scale?

> Both mentorship and governance are important parts of a sustainable ecosystem. I tune my lens on those facets of open source every day with new information and experience.

## What do you hope attendees will take away from your keynote?

> The system we're building to support marginalized people in tech is working and that's why its a target. Sometimes in systems that intersect with capitalism, the cruelty is the point. 

## Where can attendees follow you or learn more about your work?

> [dawnwages.info](http://dawnwages.info/) ! I'm blogging more too :)
